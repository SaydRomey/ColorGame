1) Affinity threshold analysis

The Hue Affinity Tiers doc is the first real scaffold for making affinity concrete. Each hue has its own meter, and tier breakpoints unlock:

powers,

dialogue,

relic scaling,

secret branches,

and eventually ending-level content. 

Hue Affinity Tiers

What is strong here

The tier model is clean and already aligned with the rest of the project:

Initiate = basic use and flavor recognition.

Adept = meaningful gameplay utility and fuller social recognition.

Expert = advanced combo systems and hidden/lore-facing content.

EXPERT = endgame identity expression and exclusive branches. 

Hue Affinity Tiers

That works because it maps directly onto your broader system split:

bound hue = current toolset,

affinity tier = long-term attunement,

personality = how that attunement is expressed in tone and perception.

Best design choice in the doc

The best part is that affinity XP comes from multiple sources:

ability usage,

time bound,

region exposure,

lore discovery,

item interaction. 

Hue Affinity Tiers

That is correct for this project. It prevents affinity from feeling like a pure grind meter and makes it reflect how the player inhabits a hue, not just how often they press its attack button.

What still needs definition

The largest missing piece is still the actual threshold model:

whether X₁/X₂/X₃ are shared across hues,

whether some hues level faster/slower,

whether decay exists,

and how current loadout vs historical investment interact. 

Hue Affinity Tiers

My read is that the system wants uniform base thresholds with hue-specific content density, not hue-specific XP curves. That would keep it readable.

Best interpretation of how tiers should function

Based on all files so far, the strongest model would be:

Initiate: the world notices your tint.

Adept: the world starts trusting or distrusting you on that basis.

Expert: hidden systems, relic synergies, and NPC confidences unlock.

EXPERT: you can speak or act with that hue’s deepest authority.

That would fit both your scene gating and your endgame rhetoric.
