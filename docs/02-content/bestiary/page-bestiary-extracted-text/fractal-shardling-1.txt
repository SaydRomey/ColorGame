FRACTAL SHARDLING

RECURSION MADE SHARP

“A thought. A shape.
Repeating until it breaks.”

“HIT IT ONCE, AND IT REMEMBERS FOREVER.”

IDENTIFICATION

Name: Fractal Shardling

Type: Recursive Entity / Prism Construct

Threat Level: Moderate → Escalating

First Emerged: Post-Shattering Splinterfall

CORE TRAITS

1. Floating prisms shaped like broken math.

2. They loop movement in recursive spirals, obeying no natural gravity.

3. Every strike splinters them — not into pieces, but smaller selves.

4. When struck, each Shardling splits into two smaller, faster versions.

5. Gaining speed and unpredictability with every generation.

6. Recursion limit: usually five generations.

7. At the limit, they collapse inward with a silent burst.

8. Collapse can alter terrain geometry or shift level logic.

HUE INTERACTION

Changes color slightly with each split.

Mimics hue gradients but never fully displays them.

Immune to direct hue magic.

Light-based illusions, especially Yellow and Violet, delay recursion.

BEHAVIOR LOOP

Patrols fixed paths until disturbed.

Targets the last source of impact.

Each smaller version emits a fainter hum.

Eventually becomes silent, but more dangerous.

RECURSIVE SPLIT PATTERN

One split into two.

Two split into four.

Five generations max.

Final state: inward collapse.

KNOWN WEAKNESSES

Perfectly timed sound burst, such as Echo Pulse, can halt them early.

Violet’s Glamour Pulse confuses recursion, forcing false splits.

Reflecting their movement back into themselves using mirror terrain causes instant collapse.

LORE ENTRY

“They fractured the Core, hoping each splinter might forget what the whole once knew.
Instead, it remembered too well.”

“Repeated. Echoed.
Over and over.”

“A Shardling does not die.
It subdivides — each generation forgetting more... and hurting more.”

“Only one truth breaks recursion:
memory without repetition.”

— Fragmented scrawl found etched on a Nullshade monolith.

FIELD NOTES / FINAL OBSERVATIONS

If more than five are allowed to split, level geometry may deform.
Floating platforms may misalign, and doors may shift location.

Occasionally whispers math-like patterns:
“One becomes two. Two becomes... error.”

1 → 2 → 4 → 8 → 16 → ? → ∞ → error
