COQUELICOT BANNER

SYMBOL OF RESISTANCE;
A VIVID POPPY STANDARD CARRIED BY FORGOTTEN HEROES

The Coquelicot Banner is more than cloth and thread—it is memory given shape.
Woven in the blood-red of sacrifice and the black of grief, it rises wherever the oppressed gather courage to stand.

It does not command.
It reminds.

THEY SPEAK IN REMEMBRANCE

“Poppies bloom where martyrs fall.”

“Raise a flag, bury a name.
It’s the same gesture.”

“When the wind lifts the banner, it remembers who held it.”

“No grave marks me.
The flowers do.”

CONTEXTUAL LINE

When giving the brooch to the player:

“This doesn’t make you a hero.
It makes you the next.”

THE BANNER’S DUTIES

RALLY OF THE FALLEN
Inspires the weary and the forgotten to rise again in defiance of tyranny.

REMEMBRANCE
Honors the lives lost to injustice, so their sacrifice is never undone.

SIGN OF RESISTANCE
Unites scattered hearts under a symbol that cannot be erased.

VOICE OF THE SILENCED
Carries the hopes of those who have no voice and no grave.

BLOOM OF DEFIANCE
Poppies remind all that beauty and blood often grow from the same earth.

KEY ITEM

COQUELICOT BROOCH

Worn by war widows; summons a field of ghostly poppies once per year.

Calls forth a spectral field of poppies.

May be used only once per year.

Ghostly petals confuse foes and fortify allies.

Honors the fallen, strengthens the living.

“The dead do not need a voice.
They need someone to remember.”

RITES AND MEANINGS OF THE POPPY

BLOOD BLOOM
Borne into battle to honor those who will not return.

VEIL OF GRIEF
Worn in mourning to cloak sorrow in silent pride.

FIELD OF NAMES
Poppies planted over the unmarked, the unknown.

FLAME PETAL
Burned as incense to carry names on the smoke.

REMEMBRANCE DAY
A day of silence, where banners fly half-mast.

WHERE TO FIND IT

BATTLEFIELDS OF VIREYA
Where old wars gave everything, the banner may still be found—torn, buried, waiting.

THE WIDOWS’ CIRCLE
In hidden villages, widows keep the banner safe until it is needed again.

THE RED FIELDS
Where poppies grow wild over forgotten graves, the banner listens.

“WE DO NOT FIGHT FOR GLORY.
WE FIGHT SO THEY ARE NOT FORGOTTEN.”

They say the first Coquelicot Banner was sewn by a mother who had lost all three of her sons.
She stitched each petal with their names, and each name with a prayer.

Now the banner belongs to no one, and to everyone.

As long as one heart remembers, it will never fall.
