
# Hue-Affinity Analysis and notes

---

## Affinity threshold analysis

The Hue Affinity Tiers doc is the first real scaffold for making affinity concrete.  

Each hue has its own meter, and tier breakpoints unlock:
- powers,
- dialogue,
- relic scaling,
- secret branches,
- and eventually ending-level content.
- Hue Affinity Tiers
- What is strong here

The tier model is clean and already aligned with the rest of the project:
- Initiate = basic use and flavor recognition.
- Adept = meaningful gameplay utility and fuller social recognition.
- Expert = advanced combo systems and hidden/lore-facing content.
- EXPERT = endgame identity expression and exclusive branches.
- Hue Affinity Tiers

That works because it maps directly onto your broader system split:
- bound hue = current toolset,
- affinity tier = long-term attunement,
- personality = how that attunement is expressed in tone and perception.
- Best design choice in the doc

The best part is that affinity XP comes from multiple sources:
- ability usage,
- time bound,
- region exposure,
- lore discovery,
- item interaction.
- Hue Affinity Tiers

That is correct for this project.  
It prevents affinity from feeling like a pure grind meter 
and makes it reflect how the player inhabits a hue, 
not just how often they press its attack button.

---

## What still needs definition

The largest missing piece is still the actual threshold model:
- whether X₁/X₂/X₃ are shared across hues,
- whether some hues level faster/slower,
- whether decay exists,
- and how current loadout vs historical investment interact.
- Hue Affinity Tiers

My read is that the system wants uniform base thresholds 
with hue-specific content density, not hue-specific XP curves.  
That would keep it readable.

## Best interpretation of how tiers should function

Based on all files so far, the strongest model would be:
- Initiate: the world notices your tint.
- Adept: the world starts trusting or distrusting you on that basis.
- Expert: hidden systems, relic synergies, and NPC confidences unlock.
- EXPERT: you can speak or act with that hue’s deepest authority.
- That would fit both your scene gating and your endgame rhetoric.

## What the scenes reveal about the affinity threshold problem

The scenes help clarify how affinity gating should work in practice.

### Gate types:

#### Bound-hue gate
You need the hue equipped now.

Examples:
- specific active abilities,
- combat counters,
- traversal tools,
- ritual triggers.

#### Affinity-tier gate
You need enough long-term attunement to a hue.

Examples:
- alternate branches,
- shrine authorization,
- true-name or true-color recognition,
- secret dialogue,
- ending lines.
- Hue Affinity Tiers
- Whispers Beneath the Greenwood

#### C. Resonance gate
You do not strictly need the hue, but having it unlocks the fuller/truer version.

Examples:
- optional Violet in “The Song That Grew Thorns,”
- optional support uses in traversal/combat,
- likely alternate Memory Echo detail.

## Model for affinity thresholds

- Initiate
  - NPCs notice your tint.
  - You get basic hue flavor lines.
  - Minor relics activate weakly.
  - Some scene objects respond to you.
  - Hue Affinity Tiers
- Adept
  - You pass local shrine and archive checks.
  - You gain standard regional trust or suspicion.
  - Base relic functionality unlocks.
  - Secondary route variants and some persuasion branches open.
  - Hue Affinity Tiers
- Expert
  - You access “true” versions of certain Memory Echoes.
  - Hidden NPC confessions unlock.
  - Hybrid mechanics and scene reinterpretations appear.
  - Relics gain bonus behavior.
  - Hue Affinity Tiers
- Master
  - You can invoke deep-name or final-form hue authority.
  - Exclusive epilogues/endings and major faction outcomes unlock.
  - Some scenes may invert, deepen, or reveal hidden historical truth.
